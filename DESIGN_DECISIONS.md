# Design decisions, critiques and open questions

*Draft, 2026-10-21.* For each pipeline: the decisions made, why, the strongest critique a reviewer could raise, and what evidence exists or what test would answer it. Evidence tags: **[have]** we already have data, **[partial]** some evidence, **[none]** untested, **[cheap]** an inexpensive test exists. Numbers come from the runs in `data/` (see PLAN.md). Where a claim is an assumption, it says so.

## Attrition at a glance (report this in the thesis)
300 sampled domains -> 268 with a usable archived homepage -> 81 qualifying (allowed category, English, confidence >= 0.6, passes viability) -> 30 drawn -> 37 checked (replacements included) -> **28 usable sites**.

## Pipeline 1: finding the websites

**1.1 Source: Wayback snapshots from 2021 or earlier.** *Why:* the original must predate AI site generation so it is human-made. *Critique:* the original is a 2021 human baseline, so any difference confounds "AI style" with "2024+ design trends". No post-2023 human control exists (declined). Wayback itself is survivorship-biased (what got archived, and whether assets survived). *Questions:* how much of the generated-vs-original difference is simply era? *Evidence:* [none]. Options: a post-2023 human control sample; or restrict claims to "relative to 2021 designs".

**1.2 Unpopular sites: Tranco ranks > 100,000.** *Why:* reduce the chance the model memorised the site. *Critique:* (a) the cutoff is arbitrary; (b) unpopular does not mean unseen: Common Crawl, a likely training source, covers the long tail; (c) low-ranked sites differ systematically (simpler, older, template-based), so results may not hold for popular sites; (d) Tranco only ranks about 1M domains, so the unranked tail is missing. *Questions:* did the generator recognise any site? Does the cutoff matter? *Evidence:* [none] the contamination probe is built (`3_annotate/contamination_probe.py`, about $1) but not run; a sensitivity check at a different rank band would answer (b)-(c).

**1.3 Random sample of 300 domains, then LLM category labels, then a draw balanced by category.** *Why:* the aim is a spread across purposes, not a population estimate. *Critique:* balanced allocation does not describe the web; with 1-3 sites per category, category claims are anecdotal; rare categories could not be filled (Ads, Information Technology, Miscellaneous, Religion, Weather had 0 qualifying; Real Estate ended at 0). *Questions:* is category the right stratifier, given that you originally cared about purpose (personal, company, academic)? Why 2 per category? *Evidence:* [have] counts by category; [none] power for category effects, which is essentially nil at this n.

**1.4 Cloudflare's 26 top-level categories, applied by an LLM to the 2021 page.** *Why:* a published, citable taxonomy with a broad top level, in place of my own list. *Critique:* it is a topic and safety taxonomy (the first 12-class purpose list was dropped), the labels are the LLM's, not Cloudflare's, and a reader cannot reproduce them; the labeller is the same model family as the generator; the labels were never reviewed (a few look wrong: `widro.com`, `thync.com`, `cbdoilcreamnk.com`). *Questions:* how accurate are the labels? Would a different prompt or model change the category of many sites? *Evidence:* [none] [cheap] hand-label a sample of about 50, and relabel with a second model to get agreement.

**1.5 Excluding six categories (Adult Themes, Child Abuse, Gambling, Questionable Content, Security Risks, Violence).** *Why:* ethics and safety of generating content. *Critique:* these are large parts of the real web, so the sample is not "websites in general"; spam and parked domains also fall out via the labeller. *Evidence:* [have] about a quarter of an earlier labelled pool was spam or unusable. State this as a scope limit.

**1.6 English only (labeller says `en` AND Lingua >= 0.90 of the extracted copy).** *Why:* prompts and analysis in one language. *Critique:* most of the web is not English, so conclusions are about English-language sites; the detector misreads short fragments (it labelled "Book a demo" as Sotho); HTML comments in the copy once polluted the scores, found late and fixed. *Questions:* does dropping non-English pages bias design (typography, density)? Is 0.90 right? *Evidence:* [partial] the threshold was checked against 52 human keep/exclude verdicts: no cutoff got fewer than 9 of 52 wrong, so 0.90 is a reasonable but not sharp choice.

**1.7 Viability filters: snapshot <= 2021, HTTP 200, still at the site root, >= 150 words of static copy, >= 1 heading, >= 2 images, HTML <= 700KB.** *Why:* the generator needs real copy and images. *Critique:* selects static, image-rich, text-heavy pages; JavaScript-only sites (a large share of the modern web) are excluded; the filters correlate with design. *Questions:* what share of attrition is due to each filter, and does it differ by category? *Evidence:* [partial] the exclusion reasons are logged (`data/sites_check.csv`, `exclusions.csv`) but not summarised by category [cheap].

**1.8 Draw rule: 2 per fulfilled category, then topped up to 30 with the single sites of partial categories and 3 extras from the largest categories.** *Why:* reach about 30 from the existing pool without new sourcing. *Critique:* the top-up is ad hoc and breaks equal allocation (3 each in Business, Shopping, Technology); replacements are chosen after seeing viability, which biases toward sites with many images. *Evidence:* [have] the rule and reasons are recorded per site in `sites_draw_cf.csv` (`reason` column).

## Pipeline 2: scraping / skeletonizing, then vibe-coding

**2.1 Input = the page's copy and images only (no CSS, layout, fonts or screenshot).** *Why:* the agent should design, not copy a design. *Critique (the biggest one, and an advisor raised it):* images carry design (screenshots with annotations, logos, photos set the palette), the section order is the original designer's layout, and link addresses reveal structure. So "what the agent comes up with on its own" is conditioned on the original. We kept images and verbatim copy by choice. *Questions:* how much is inherited versus invented? *Evidence:* [partial] accent hue of the generated page is closer to the original's than chance (median 34 degrees vs 93 expected, p = 0.034). [none] [cheap] an ablation on a subset: images removed, or copy blocks shuffled, to measure the effect.

**2.2 Sanitize rules** (remove placement labels, all image alt text, divider rules, unreferenced images; keep file names, link addresses, emphasis, headings). *Why:* strip explicit design clues without destroying content. *Critique:* the scrub is partial and its choices untested (file names such as `hero` or `footer-graph` still describe roles; emphasis and heading levels are design-adjacent); different sites are scrubbed to different degrees. *Questions:* does any clue change the output? *Evidence:* [none].

**2.3 Skeletonizer heuristics** (real image source from lazy-load attributes, backgrounds from stylesheets, `<style>` and inline styles; skip hidden elements with media queries evaluated at 1440px; boilerplate by class-name regex; mobile-only sources dropped). *Why:* the first pilot lost 7 of 9 photos and included hidden overlay text. *Critique:* heuristic and untested at scale: the boilerplate regex is a substring match; CSS specificity is ignored; a stylesheet Wayback cannot serve disables hiding and background capture; tables flatten; the copy of the 28 sites was not hand-reviewed. *Questions:* how faithful is the skeleton to what the page shows? *Evidence:* [none] [cheap] compare each skeleton's text with the original page's rendered text (`original/text.txt`) for precision and recall.

**2.4 Usable-input caps: 150-3000 words, 2-40 referenced images.** *Why:* stubs and index pages are poor inputs (one candidate had 24k words and 389 images). *Critique:* arbitrary; excludes both thin and huge sites; copy length turned out to drive results (more copy, more features present, rho 0.63). *Evidence:* [have] the driver correlation, so copy length should be a covariate.

**2.5 Generator: Claude Code (`claude -p`, Sonnet 5.5), prompt "Build a website landing page from this content...", no style guidance, no format instruction, fresh directory, one run per site.** *Why:* observe defaults; let the stack be an outcome. *Critiques:*
- **The prompt says "landing page".** That alone invites hero, CTA and card-grid tropes. Real users prompt differently. *Test:* [none] [cheap] a few prompt variants on a subset.
- **One model, one run.** Findings describe this agent; run-to-run noise is unmeasured (could be large). *Test:* [none] repeat runs on a subset (5 sites x 3 runs: about $3 for generation, about $13 with both judges).
- **One-shot, no visual feedback.** The agent never saw its page; real vibe coding iterates while looking at the result.
- **Ecological validity of the stack.** All 28 agents built a single static HTML file, whereas in-the-wild tools (Lovable, Bolt) default to React and Tailwind. Our environment (a plain directory, shell access) may have driven that. *Evidence:* [have] 27 plain static HTML, 1 with Alpine.js from a CDN.

**2.6 Render and measure: Playwright at 1440px, full-page screenshot, DOM facts, visible text; whatever the agent built is auto-detected, built if needed, and served locally.** *Why:* uniform, comparable pages. *Critique:* desktop only; static screenshot (no hover, animation or scroll behaviour); the original's webfonts are missing from the prompt, so font differences mix preference with missing assets (counted as a possible signature by choice). *Evidence:* [partial] format detection tested on synthetic outputs and on the 28 real ones.

**2.7 Copy fidelity check REMOVED (2026-10-26).** The check asked whether each prompt sentence appears in the rendered page. It is dropped because which copy the agent keeps is itself the behaviour being studied, not an error to detect. Existing `fidelity.json` files stay on disk, unused. Original note: a check that the agent kept the content. *Critique:* measures presence, not placement; text hidden by default counts as missing; mean fidelity is 76% (range 33-100%), so some pages are partly rewritten or trimmed: a selection effect on every downstream feature. *Evidence:* [have] per-site fidelity (old runs only), a possible measure of copy selection if wanted later.

**2.8 Wayback access: raw `id_` and `im_` URLs, throttled, retried, resumable; replacements from the same category; the first check run archived after the skeleton fixes.** *Critique:* assets for one page can come from different capture dates; stale results had to be thrown away once, which shows the pipeline is sensitive to skeleton changes. *Evidence:* [have] logs and archive.

## Pipeline 3: annotation

**3.1 Two LLM judges (Sonnet and Opus) with programmatic page facts, free-form differences, no rubric, neutral instruction.** *Why:* avoid forcing a taxonomy; ground claims in facts. *Critiques:* same model family as the generator (shared blind spots, possible self-preference); the judges see original (A) and generated (B) together, so observations emphasise change, not absolute traits; always A first (order bias); screenshots are downscaled; free-form output gives inconsistent vocabulary and uneven attention; a judge lists only what it notices. *Questions:* would a non-Claude judge find different things? *Evidence:* [partial] the two judges agree on about 96% of observations, which shows consistency, not validity. [none] a judge from another model family (Gemini or GPT) on a subset.

**3.2 Confidence tiers (A/B/C) from judge agreement and an LLM fact check, instead of human grading.** *Why:* too many observations (2,592) to hand-grade. *Critique:* agreement between two same-family models can share an error; the fact check is also an LLM, and only 56% of claims are fact-checkable; tier A means "both said it", not "true". *Questions:* what is the real precision of tier A? *Evidence:* [partial] 2,263 A, 303 B, 26 C; 1.8% of checkable claims contradicted; the only human evidence is Phase 1 (one site): judge accuracy 35/38 and 31/36 early, 38/39 and 42/42 once full facts were given. [none] [cheap] hand-grade about 100 tier-A observations to estimate precision.

**3.3 Feature catalog: per-site LLM extraction, then LLM grouping per family; seed sites only; programmatic proxies suggested by the LLM.** *Why:* discover the feature set from the data. *Critiques:* grouping is opaque and not reproducible; families are fixed in advance, producing cross-family duplicates ("rounded corners on cards" appears under decoration and component); granularity is arbitrary (188 features for 28 sites; 36% appear on one site); features reflect what judges tend to notice (visual, structural), not everything; no inter-coder reliability. *Questions:* would a second extraction run produce the same catalog? Is a feature diagnostic or merely descriptive? *Evidence:* [none] [cheap] repeat the grouping and measure feature overlap.

**3.4 Adequacy test: 24 discovery / 4 hold-out sites over 20 seeded splits; thresholds (plateau, singleton share, hold-out coverage >= 80%).** *Why:* test whether the catalog generalizes beyond the sites it came from. *Critiques:* feature IDs were assigned once over all sites, so merging decisions can leak across the split (an approximation of per-split rediscovery); 4 hold-out sites is noisy; thresholds are arbitrary; coverage is not validity; discovery and hold-out share the same judges' biases. *Evidence:* [have] hold-out coverage 0.90 (0.82-0.98) but no plateau (about 2.8 new features per added site), a mixed readout.

**3.5 Detection: programmatic measurements wherever a proxy exists, else a vision checklist; a proxy that agrees with the checklist on < 70% of pages falls back to the checklist.** *Why:* measurable features should not depend on an LLM's eyes. *Critiques:* the proxies and their thresholds were proposed by an LLM (agreement only 0.73; 9 of 38 failed and fell back), so detection is not uniform across features; the checklist is the same model family, reads downscaled screenshot slices, and answered "unclear" for about 10% of cells; there is no ground truth. *Questions:* are checklist answers repeatable? Would calibrated thresholds fix the proxies? *Evidence:* [partial] [cheap] run the checklist twice on a subset for test-retest; calibrate proxy thresholds on the originals.

**3.6 Variation analysis: prevalence with Wilson intervals, Jaccard similarity, measured-style spread vs originals, paired Wilcoxon tests (BH-corrected), inheritance, drivers.** *Why:* describe sharedness and homogenization. *Critiques:* n = 28 (intervals of about +/-18 points); the style-spread comparison uses 12 hand-picked measurements, z-scored together; some "universal" features are artifacts of every agent choosing single-file static HTML (minimal markup, no external assets); category effects are untestable at 1-3 sites each; the paired tests use one run per site. *Questions:* is homogenization sensitive to the choice of measurements? *Evidence:* [have] the numbers (spread 3.46 vs 4.71, difference -1.25, CI -2.06 to -0.24); [cheap] repeat with a different measurement set.

**3.8 Embedding-based merging of observation sheets into a master list, then collages (added 2026-10-21; a second aggregation method alongside 3.3).** *What:* each site's list of extracted observations is a "sheet"; every observation is embedded; the two most similar sheets (by mutual-nearest-neighbour matches at cosine >= 0.78) are merged, matched observations becoming one entry whose site set is the union; repeat until one master sheet remains; a final pass merges near-duplicates (>= 0.85); the result is a master list with the number of sites sharing each entry. For each shared entry, a vision call locates the example on each relevant page and the crops are tiled into one collage per observation. *Decision on the embedding model (user-approved):* **fastembed with BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5**, run locally on ONNX (about 150-200 MB with no PyTorch), over the two alternatives offered: model2vec/potion-base-8M (about 30-40 MB, literal static word embeddings, lighter but lower quality) and sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (about 300-500 MB, pulls PyTorch). Chosen for sentence-level quality at modest size; paraphrases scored 0.84 and unrelated feature statements about 0.5 in a quick check.
*Critiques and findings:*
- **Embeddings group by topic, not by value.** Entries merged "square corners" with "rounded/pill corners" and "links red and underlined" with "links blue, not underlined", so a raw count would overstate sharing. *Response:* an LLM verification pass splits each multi-site entry into groups that state the same trait and value (default on; the unverified list is kept). *Residual:* the verifier is the same model family; its splits are unaudited.
- **The threshold is arbitrary and the counts move with it.** Master entries / entries shared by >= 3 sites: tau 0.70 gives 190 / 49, 0.74 gives 226 / 45, 0.78 gives 276 / 48, 0.82 gives 401 / 40. At 0.78 about 73% of entries are singletons.
- **Greedy, order-dependent.** Pair merging is agglomerative on a similarity (Dice over matches) that is itself threshold-dependent; a different merge order or linkage could give a different master list. Not yet tested (compare against random merge orders).
- **Input quality.** The sheets are the LLM-extracted statements (733 of them), so the list inherits the extraction's wording and omissions. A raw-observation mode exists (`--source observations`) but was not run.
- **Counts are mentions, not presence.** A site "shares" an entry if its judges mentioned the trait; absence is not measured here (that is Stage 4 detection, which still uses the LLM catalog from 3.3).
- **Two aggregation methods now coexist** (LLM grouping for the catalog, embeddings for the master list). Their agreement has not been compared, which would show how method-dependent the shared-feature list is.
- **Collage locator.** Bounding boxes come from a vision model on downscaled screenshot slices; they are unverified, page-wide features get an arbitrary representative region, only visual families are cropped, and the located share is reported per feature. Collages show the examples the model chose, so they can flatter a feature.


*Results (2026-10-22):* the verification pass split 53 of 74 multi-site entries (72%), so unverified counts were inflated: master list 276 -> 430 entries, entries shared by >= 3 sites 48 -> 54, top entry 24 -> 20 sites, singletons about 76%. Verification cost $2.44 (74 calls); the collage locator cost $2.31 (28 calls) and produced 44 collages with 200 of 218 crops located. *Spot check:* two collages (eyebrow labels, pill buttons) were inspected and every crop showed the stated feature; the remaining 42 are unreviewed, so the locator's accuracy is not established. *New residual:* verification splits entries but never recombines them, so near-duplicates survive (e.g. "semantic landmark elements" appears as two entries with 9 and 7 sites; "system font stack" as 6 and 5; "descriptive alt text" as 13 and 6), which understates their true counts. A second consolidation pass over the verified entries is needed before the counts are quoted.

**3.9 Attribute-and-value coding as the main route to shared features (decided 2026-10-22, on the assistant's recommendation after the embedding merge mixed opposite values).** *What:* embeddings are used only to find TOPICS (agglomerative clustering of the 733 observation statements, cosine distance < 0.32; oversized topics re-split, singletons batched by family); for each topic an LLM defines ATTRIBUTES (a dimension on which pages differ) with closed sets of 2-7 mutually exclusive VALUES and codes each statement to attribute-value pairs; a consolidation call per family merges duplicate attributes (unified values plus a mapping); then a vision coder (screenshot slices, page text and the programmatic measurements as ground truth) assigns every page a value for every attribute, so "not mentioned" is separated from "absent". The analysis is the value distribution per attribute (modal value and share, normalised entropy). *Why:* values are explicit, so "square" and "pill" cannot merge; granularity is set by the definitions, not by a similarity threshold; the output (a value distribution per attribute) is what variation analysis needs.
*Results (28 pages):* 278 attributes (from 373 raw), 1,039 values, 1,530 observation codes; 7,724 page-coded cells (99% filled); modal-value bands: 121 shared (>= 75%), 116 split, 41 varied; of the shared, 61 are shared presence and 60 are shared absence or "other" (the modal value says the feature is absent or not applicable). Pairs of pages differ in similarity more than independent coding would give (SD 0.050 vs a null of 0.027), so there is cluster structure. Coder vs the values coded from the judges' observations agrees on 68% of 1,431 comparisons (48-73% by family; imagery and copy lowest); neither side is ground truth.
*Problems found while building it (fixed; kept here because they changed results):* (1) my filter that removes "not stated" style values used the pattern `n/?a`, which matches any "na" inside a word, and wrongly removed real values (blue/navy, dominant...) from 35 attributes and left 10 with fewer than 2 values, forcing answers; those were recoded. (2) Attributes that apply to only some pages had no way to say "absent", so the coder was forced to name a value on every page (e.g. "announcement bar height = thin single strip" on all 28 pages); 72 such attributes got an explicit `not_applicable` value and were recoded. (3) The first "more alike than chance" test was empty: shuffling values within each attribute cannot change the mean pairwise agreement, so observed equalled the null; it is replaced by a test of the spread of pairwise agreement. The first results (133 shared attributes, "not distinguishable from chance") were therefore wrong. These bugs were found by checking implausible outputs, which suggests every aggregate should be sanity-checked before it is quoted.
*Critiques:* attributes, value sets and the "shared >= 0.75" cut are LLM-made or arbitrary, and closed value sets can force a poor choice or a catch-all "other" (about 12% of cells); the codebook is built from the same 28 sites it then describes, with no hold-out (the earlier hold-out test applied to the statement catalog, not to this codebook); the coder is the same model family as the generator and sees downscaled slices; there is one coder, so the only reliability figure is agreement with judge-derived values, which mixes coder error with errors in the earlier statement-to-attribute coding; whether a modal value counts as an "absence" is decided by a regex heuristic; 57 attributes were defined from one site's statement and so have narrow value sets; "shared" counts depend on how finely attributes are cut (more attributes inflate "varied"). *Evidence still missing:* a human check of a sample of coded cells, a second coder (different model family), and a hold-out test of the codebook on new sites.
*Exhaustive galleries:* the first collages (3.8) were not exhaustive: one crop per site, only sites a judge mentioned (the eyebrow-label gallery showed 8 sites; the coding says 24 pages have eyebrow labels), 44 observations only. `attribute_galleries.py` now builds one gallery per attribute value from the complete coded matrix: every page coded with the value, up to 6 located instances per page, paginated contact sheets, with a coverage report. *Caveats:* boxes come from a vision model and are unverified (two test sheets were accurate and showed every instance on the page); page-level properties get one representative region flagged page-wide; the cap of 6 instances per page can truncate; no precision check has been run.

**3.10 Evidence at coding time, replacing the separate locator (decided and run 2026-10-23, on the user's suggestion that annotation already needs evidence from each page).** *What:* the coder cites its evidence in the same call as the value. `element_map.py` re-renders each page (1440 px, local server) and numbers its visible elements (id, parent, tag, text, pixel box; wrapper elements with one same-sized child are dropped; at most about 450 per page); `code_pages_evidence.py` receives the screenshot slices (cut from the same render), page text, measurements, the element list and the closed value sets, and returns per attribute `{value, evidence: [ids], page_wide}`; `evidence_galleries.py` crops each cited element's real box with context (at least 120 px sideways, 80 px vertically) and a red outline. Galleries are a join on (site, attribute, value), so consolidating or re-merging attributes later needs no new LLM calls. *Why:* a separate locator is a second, independent guess about where the first call's claim applies, which is why 391 pairs were unlocated, half the eyebrow pages were missing and some crops were wrong; and a value with no cited evidence is now a visible low-confidence signal instead of an ambiguous gap. *Alternatives:* keep the locator and add a precision pass (more calls, and still two guesses); pixel boxes from the model (imprecise: the old crops clipped headings); an evidence-only pass over the old codes (brings the second guess back).

*Results (28 pages):* pilot on 3 pages ($2.25), then all 28 ($20.45 in total, 142 calls; two JSON-parse failures retried). 4,638 of 4,852 non-absence (page, value) pairs cite evidence (96%; decoration 100%, color 98%, typography and layout 97%, component 95%, imagery 94%, semantic_dom 91%, copy 90%); 9,472 instance crops, 610 galleries (126 MB), 13 galleries with no crop (was 17). Eyebrow labels above headings: 18 of 18 coded pages, 55 instances (the locator: 10 of 20 pages, 25 instances). *Spot check:* one gallery sheet and the 15-crop pilot sheet were inspected; nearly all crops are the stated feature, a few are arguable (a "FOLLOW US" footer heading and a "COMING SOON!" badge counted as eyebrows; tiny white labels on photos are hard to read). No precision rate has been measured.

*Second coding run as a test-retest:* the evidence run recoded all cells with the same model and a different prompt, so agreement with the first run estimates coding noise: 83% of 7,667 cells agree (typography 88%, component 84%, layout and color 83%, decoration 83%, semantic_dom 79%, imagery 77%, copy 74%). On the attribute level, 241 of 278 attributes keep the same modal value, 222 keep the same band, mean absolute change in modal share is 0.063, and the shared set (modal share >= 0.75) is 121 vs 118 attributes with 102 in both (Jaccard 0.74). The re-run bands are 118 shared / 116 split / 44 varied; pairwise-agreement SD 0.047 vs null 0.027 (structure persists); coder-vs-judge agreement rose from 68% to 72% (copy 56%, imagery 55% lowest). *Reading:* about a fifth of attributes sit near a band threshold and flip between runs, so a shared-feature list from one run should not be quoted as exact; the robust list is the intersection of both runs.

*Critiques:* the cited evidence comes from the same model that made the claim, so it makes errors visible but is not independent confirmation; element ids can only point at DOM elements, so values that live inside an image (text baked into a photo) cannot be cited (most of the 214 real-valued codes without evidence are semantic_dom and copy); the evidence prompt changed the coding prompt too, so the test-retest mixes prompt and sampling noise; the analysis still treats a code as one unit although a page can show a feature in some sections and not others; the originals have not been coded, so there is still no lift. The old locator code and its galleries (`attribute_galleries.py`, `data/analysis/galleries/`, 82 MB) are kept for comparison and could be deleted. *Corrected costs:* page coding $22.39 including recodes (quoted $14.60), first gallery locator $13.40 (quoted about $11); all-time ledger $109.84, pipeline 3 about $99.1.

**3.7 Deferred on purpose: lift against the originals, run-to-run noise, contamination probe, signature thresholds, human grading.** *Critique:* without lift there is no claim yet about what is *specific to AI*, only what the AI pages share; without noise estimates the shared-feature list may include chance. These are the main gaps before any thesis claim.

## Questions to answer first (ranked by threat to validity and cost)
0a. **Is the attribute-value codebook reliable?** Test-retest within one model is now known (83% of cells, 80% of attributes keep their band, 3.10); still needed: hand-check about 100 coded cells and about 50 gallery crops, a second coder from another model family, and hold-out sites. [cheap]
0. **Do the two aggregation methods agree, and is the master list stable?** Compare the embedding master list with the LLM catalog, and vary tau and merge order. [cheap]
1. **Does the "landing page" prompt shape the output?** Try 2-3 neutral variants on a subset. [cheap]
2. **How much does the look come from the images and section order?** Ablate images and order on a subset. [cheap]
3. **What is the real precision of tier-A observations?** Hand-grade about 100. [cheap]
4. **Did the generator recognise the sites?** Run the contamination probe. [about $1]
5. **How big is run-to-run noise?** Repeat 5 sites x 3 runs. [about $13 including both judges]
6. **Do conclusions depend on the model family?** A non-Claude judge and, ideally, a second generator.
7. **Era confound:** how to separate AI style from post-2021 design trends (control sample or a scoped claim).
8. **Label and language quality:** hand-check about 50 category labels and the English filter.
