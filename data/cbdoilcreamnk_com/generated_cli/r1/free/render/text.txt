CBD and the UK Food Standards Agency

CBD oil is everywhere on social media, in food, drinks and beauty products. It comes from the cannabis plant and is claimed to help with conditions such as anxiety or pain, offering the calming effects of marijuana without the unwanted "freakier" side effects.

The UK's Food Standards Agency (FSA) has now cautioned against CBD. People on medication, breastfeeding mothers and pregnant women are advised not to eat CBD-infused food.
The FSA warned that CBD-infused products could be banned from UK shops if their makers don't provide more detailed safety information.
FSA Chief Executive Emily Miles said many products sold on the high street were not properly authorised and were taking a risky gamble.
The CBD industry must give the regulator more information about the safety and contents of its products before 31 March 2021. Otherwise the products will be taken off the shelves. The quote was reported by The Guardian.
Miles described the current measures as a practical, proportionate step that balances protecting public health with consumer choice.
Deadline: 31 March 2021, the date by which the industry must supply the FSA with safety and content information.
